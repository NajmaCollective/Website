About Najma

A teacher-led collective for online English lessons

Najma is a teacher-led collective offering online English lessons for adults and organisations. We welcome anyone who would like to study with us, while maintaining a particular connection with people involved in solidarity and social change.

Our purpose is to make good English teaching affordable while creating paid work that teachers have a direct say in shaping. Najma grew from relationships formed through Palestinian education, and those relationships continue to inform the collective.

Where Najma came from

The teachers who began Najma had worked together through Noor Community with Palestinian learners and educators. They created Najma as a separate project for paid English teaching, with its own booking arrangements and shared decisions.

Noor Community

Noor Community began in 2024 as a volunteer-led education initiative for Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank. Its online English work brought together the teacher network from which Najma’s first cohort is being formed. Najma operates separately from Noor. During the pilot, about four teachers from that network will take part in Najma.

[Link: Learn more about Noor Community]

Al Manar Society for Culture & Creativity

Al Manar Society for Culture & Creativity is an independent Palestinian organisation based in Aida refugee camp in Bethlehem. It works with youth, women and children through education, community activity and programmes that support participation in public life. Al Manar’s work also includes English-language education for learners in Gaza and the West Bank. This forms part of the wider Palestinian-led educational work connected with Najma’s origins.

During the pilot, Najma proposes to allocate 5% of revenue from paid services to Al Manar. The contribution and public relationship will begin once Al Manar approves the arrangement.

A collective shaped by its teachers

During the pilot, each participating teacher has one vote on shared decisions, which are agreed by a simple majority.

Teachers decide which services they offer and retain professional control over their teaching. They choose their own methods and materials within the shared pilot prices and booking terms.

How we approach learning

You and your teacher decide what you want from your lessons. You may want to improve your English over time or prepare for something more immediate, such as a university assignment or an important meeting. The teaching can develop as your interests and priorities change.

Each teacher draws on their experience to choose activities and materials that suit the work you have agreed together. Their feedback helps you understand what you are doing well and where further practice would be useful.

As you continue, later lessons can build on the progress you have made. You can stay with the same teacher or book another Najma teacher when your schedule or interests change, while keeping a connection with your earlier learning.

What Najma offers

You can book private English lessons for yourself or arrange a programme for your organisation. The Solidarity Café offers another way to meet Najma through conversations about solidarity and social change, with free attendance during the pilot.

Private lessons

Work individually with a teacher on the English you use in everyday life or in your studies and work. Specialist lessons, including IELTS preparation, are available through teachers with relevant experience.

Programmes for organisations

Start with a free consultation about how your staff or members use English. Programmes can include specialist English for advocacy and organising as well as business English and professional communication training.

Group sessions cost £10 per learner for 55 minutes, with a minimum of four learners. Private lessons cost £20 per learner for 55 minutes.

Solidarity Café

The Café is free during the pilot. Join an online conversation about solidarity and social change, usually lasting 55–75 minutes. Intermediate English, around B1 or above, is recommended.

Where Najma’s income goes

During the pilot, 80% of revenue from each paid Najma service goes to the teacher or lead teacher delivering it. Four 5% shares support operations, co-administration, Solidarity Café facilitation and the proposed contribution to Al Manar.

  • 80% — Teaching or programme delivery
  • 5% — Operations
  • 5% — Co-administration
  • 5% — Solidarity Café facilitation
  • 5% — Proposed contribution to Al Manar Society for Culture & Creativity

These shares are calculated after refunds and before payment-processing charges. The person whose account receives the payment covers those charges.

Starting small

Najma’s pilot is designed to run for three months with about four teachers. Working on this scale gives the collective time to try the arrangements and learn from the people taking part.

We will review the pilot after six weeks and again at the end. The reviews will consider private lesson bookings and teacher income alongside organisational programmes. Café attendance and participants’ feedback will also inform the discussion, including any concerns or complaints.

The final review will help the collective decide what to change before making longer-term arrangements.
